# 10 Agba Reasoning

## Purpose

Agba's reasoning layer converts trusted company evidence into explicit, traceable understanding.

It does not replace source data. It does not invent facts. It does not treat a model response as truth.

The reasoning chain is:

```text
Reports + company data
        ↓
Normalized evidence
        ↓
Facts / observations
        ↓
Patterns and changes
        ↓
Issues / risks / opportunities
        ↓
Decisions and recommended actions
        ↓
Briefing
```

## Core rules

1. Evidence before conclusion.
2. Every material conclusion must have supporting evidence.
3. Agba distinguishes facts, observations, hypotheses, and recommendations.
4. Confidence must be explicit when inference is involved.
5. Missing information is surfaced as missing information, never silently filled.
6. Conflicting evidence remains visible until resolved.
7. Agba must not create a decision on behalf of the CEO or Department Head unless explicitly authorized by the product flow.
8. Department Heads receive reasoning within their permitted department scope. The CEO receives company-wide reasoning.
9. Repeated observations can strengthen a pattern, but repetition alone does not prove causality.
10. Historical records are never silently rewritten.

## Reasoning objects

### Fact

A directly supported normalized record, such as a reported revenue amount or expense.

### Observation

An interpretation supported by one or more facts, such as "delivery expense increased for three consecutive reporting periods."

### Issue

A material condition requiring attention, such as a payment delay affecting cash collection.

### Decision

A recorded organizational choice. Agba may capture, summarize, and track decisions. It should not silently manufacture them.

### Recommendation

A proposed action generated from evidence. Recommendations must be clearly labeled as recommendations.

## Evidence graph

Every observation, issue, recommendation, and decision generated by Agba should retain links to the evidence that supports it.

```text
Report
  ├── Metric
  ├── Expense
  ├── Task
  └── Event
       ↓
   Observation
       ↓
      Issue
       ↓
 Recommendation
```

This creates an audit path from a briefing statement back to the originating report.

## Confidence

V1 uses a simple confidence vocabulary:

- `high`: directly supported or strongly corroborated.
- `medium`: reasonable inference with some uncertainty.
- `low`: plausible but materially incomplete or ambiguous.

Low-confidence conclusions should normally become questions or watch items rather than hard assertions.

## Temporal reasoning

Agba should reason over time, not just individual reports.

Useful comparisons include:

- current period versus previous period
- current period versus recent baseline
- repeated occurrence frequency
- overdue duration
- trend direction
- goal versus actual

A single unusual value should not automatically become a company issue.

## Contradictions

If two reports conflict, Agba should preserve both records and surface the contradiction.

Example:

```text
Finance: cash expense = ₦80,000
Operations: delivery expense = ₦120,000

Agba:
"I have conflicting delivery expense figures for today.
Which figure should be treated as final?"
```

## Reasoning output

Reasoning should produce structured internal objects first. Human language is generated from those objects afterward.

```json
{
  "type": "issue",
  "title": "Payment collection delay",
  "summary": "Two sales orders remain unpaid.",
  "confidence": "high",
  "severity": "medium",
  "evidence": ["report-entry-id-1", "report-entry-id-2"],
  "recommended_action": "Follow up with the two customers before the next reporting cycle."
}
```

## What Agba should notice

V1 reasoning should prioritize:

- unusual financial movements
- overdue or blocked tasks
- repeated operational problems
- missing reports
- conflicting figures
- goal slippage
- emerging trends
- approvals waiting on action
- material changes from recent baseline

It should not attempt to understand everything equally. Attention is a scarce resource.

## What Agba should say

Agba should communicate like an intelligent office chief, not a data warehouse.

Prefer:

> "Sales was ₦420k today, but two orders are still unpaid. The same issue appeared yesterday."

Over:

> "Revenue metric variance detected."

The underlying structured reasoning remains precise even when the presentation is conversational.

## Human oversight

Agba can observe, summarize, flag, compare, question, and recommend.

Actions that materially commit the company, approve spending, change authoritative records, or communicate externally require an explicit human action unless a future version introduces a narrowly scoped automation policy.
